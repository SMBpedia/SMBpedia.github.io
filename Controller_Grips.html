Controller Grips

Thumb Grip

Miniland using thumb grip
Thumb grip is the standard, most intuitive way to use an NES controller. With this grip, you use your left thumb to move Mario left and right, and your right thumb for A/B. B is held with the tip of the thumb, while A is pressed with the joint of the thumb. Advantages of this grip include comfortability over long sessions, low strain for general gameplay, and good control, especially for beginners. Disadvantages include slower mashing (most will mash faster with either index or middle fingers), and higher strain for faster or more demanding inputs.

Piano Grip

Yelia64 using full piano
In piano grip, you use your right hand’s index to press and hold B, while the middle finger is used for A. For the left hand, pointer is typically used for holding right, and middle is used for holding left (or more rarely, the ring finger). Advantages for this grip include maximum speed for mashing or wiggles for both directions and A/B. Disadvantages include a higher learning curve, as it typically feels quite unnatural and cramped, more hand strain on the d-pad hand due to said crampage, and reduced stability since the controller is unanchored (players of this style typically place the controller down on a surface). To deal with the downsides of this grip, players often only use full piano for parts of the game where the advantages are more substantial, and switch to using their thumb at least for the d-pad.

Half-Piano

LeKukie using half-piano
Half-piano is a hybrid grip, where the thumb is used for controlling the d-pad, while your index is used for B, and your middle finger is used for A. Advantages of this grip include faster A/B alteration, easier flicking, and mashing when compared to thumb grip or claw, intuitive d-pad controls like that of thumb grip leading to an easier learning curve, and a good balance between precision and comfort. Disadvantages include coordination between thumbs vs fingers being difficult initially, reduced d-pad precision when compared to full-piano. That being said, this grip strikes a nice middle ground in between precision for A/B, without the anchoring disadvantages or left-handed cramping associated with full piano.)

Claw Grip

Caleb_2112 Playing with Clawgrip
Claw grip is a variation of half piano that reincorporates the right thumb, but only to press A, while your pointer curls around it like a “claw” in order to press the B button. Advantages of this grip include faster mashing than thumb-only grips, easier flicks, and a more natural feel than half-piano for those used to playing games with their thumbs. On the other hand, claw grip can feel quite awkward or unnatural, increase hand strain over long periods, and result in a less stable grip if not sufficiently familiar with the playstyle.
